A free guitar amp sim and multi-effects app for macOS and Windows.

Eight neural amps, a cab with mics you can move, a full pedalboard, and Match tone: load a song, play along, and it dials in the guitar. Open source, with zero added latency.

Match a tone from a song.

Load a song, pick the section where the guitar stands out, and BellyDSP finds the closest setting it has: an amp with its Gain and tone, a pedal and a compressor if they help, a cab, and a fine correction curve after it. A recording only holds the finished sound, so it finds the nearest match among its own amps and cabs; it doesn't recover the original gear.

  1. The song

    Choose a song file and drag over the part you want. With Separate the guitar first on, it pulls the guitar out of the mix.

  2. Your DI

    Press Record and play the part along with it after a count-in, or choose a DI file. Your take lines up with the song, so it compares your notes with the recording's, one by one.

  3. The match

    It shows what it chose and how close it got. Compare the song, the match, and your current sound, level-matched, then apply it in one undo step.

Everything around the amp.

The board under the stage is three tabs in signal order: Pre FX, Amp & Cab (the amp, Gate B and the cab), and Post FX. Every pedal has its own footswitch and LED, and every tab carries a row of LEDs, one per block in it, so a closed tab still shows what's on. Click a pedal to see all its controls, drag a Pre FX or Post FX pedal along its row to reorder it, and switch a whole section off at once. [ and ] step through the tabs, and selecting a block anywhere opens its tab.

Pre FX
Gate A, a noise gate that listens to the clean guitar, with a Learn button. A compressor in a studio and a pedal style, boost and overdrive pedals modelled from their circuits, and a 9-band graphic or 5-band parametric EQ.
Amp & Cab
The eight built-in amps, picked from the shelf of minis on the stage, or your own amp-only .nam capture as a ninth. The built-ins are NAM captures trained from BellyDSP's own amp models, not captures of real amps.
Gate B after the amp, which listens to the clean guitar too and can follow Gate A.
The cab: two close mics and a stereo room mic, each an impulse response, with 21 public-domain IRs built in or your own. Load a cab pack and drag a close mic across the speaker to move it.
Post FX
EQ and compressor, a harmonizer that plays in key, a multivoicer, Bloom (bitcrusher, phaser and flanger in one), chorus, delay and reverb. Delay and reverb tails ring on when you switch them off.
Around it
Presets with eight scenes each along the bottom of the window, undo and redo, A/B compare, a tuner, tap tempo, and MIDI learn on any control. Settings holds the input level and the output, with a limiter so nothing clips.

How it's built.

C++20 on JUCE, with NeuralAmpModelerCore, the official Neural Amp Modeler engine, running the amps. Sean Snaider designed and directed it, and built it with AI coding agents (Anthropic's Claude Code) under rules every change has to pass.

Source on GitHub The build plan
Latency
None added. The one exception is opt-in: the flanger's through-zero mode delays the signal by 5 ms while it's on, and says so.
The audio thread
It never allocates or frees memory, takes a lock, or touches the disk. A test plays 14 seconds through every feature and counts all three: 0, 0, 0.
CPU
The default rig uses about 6% of a 128-sample buffer on an Apple silicon Mac, and about 15% on GitHub's 4-core Windows test machine. A CPU saver switch helps older computers.
Tests
About 8,700 automated checks, including golden tests that hold the DSP blocks to Python references and the amps to NAM core's own renderer.
Open source
AGPL-3.0-or-later. Every download is built from a commit that's public and tagged first, so the exact source of every build is on GitHub. The licence.

Credits

BellyDSP stands on these open projects. Their licences ship with the app (BellyDSP > About / licenses).

NeuralAmpModelerCore
The official engine of NAM (Neural Amp Modeler), by Steven Atkinson. It runs every amp. MIT licence.
JUCE
The framework for audio, the app window, and the interface, used under the AGPLv3.
Eigen
The linear algebra library NAM runs on. MPL 2.0.
demucs.cpp
A C++ port of Demucs, by Sevag H., which separates the guitar from a song for Match tone. MIT licence. The model's weights download from the Demucs authors' repository the first time you use it.
nlohmann/json
Reads NAM's capture files. MIT licence.
Sparkle and WinSparkle
The updaters on macOS and Windows. MIT licence.

Get BellyDSP.

Free for macOS and Windows. Install it once and it keeps itself up to date. You need an audio interface set to 48 kHz.
