## Phase 3: cab packs (movable mics)

| # | Assumption | Why | To change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | File-name keywords place IRs across the speaker: Cap / Center 0, CapEdge 0.25, CapCone 0.4, Cone 0.6, Edge 1.0. Distances read from `1in`, `2.5cm`, `10mm` and are scaled so the closest capture is 0 and the farthest 1. | The common naming conventions in IR packs; the exact numbers are a guess at how far apart those positions are. | Edit the keyword table in `CabPack.cpp`, or put a `cabpack.json` in the pack folder, which overrides names entirely. |
| C2 | If names don't say where every mic was (or two files land on one spot), the files are laid out left to right in name order, as a 1D slider, and the status says so. | Better than refusing the pack. | Refuse such packs instead. |
| C3 | A pack uses each IR's first (left) channel; the close mic's left/right setting applies only to single IR files. The room mic doesn't take packs. | Packs are close-mic captures; a stereo room with positions isn't in the plan. | Per-pack channel choice is easy to add. |
| C4 | All IRs in a pack must share one sample rate; a pack that mixes rates is refused with a message. | Morphing needs a common frequency grid. Resampling at load is possible but wasn't worth it without real packs to test. | Resample at load. |
| C5 | Minimum-phase reconstruction floors the spectrum 120 dB below its peak and gives the DC and Nyquist bins their neighbours' values. | Real cab IRs have near-exact zeros at DC and Nyquist, which otherwise leave a -60 dB error spread across the morphed IR; with the fix it's -77 dB. Neither change is audible (0 Hz, 24 kHz, -120 dB). | Nothing to decide unless a real pack misbehaves. |
| C6 | IR loudness matching is now computed exactly from the IR's K-weighted energy instead of measured with 4 s of white noise. It agrees with the measurement within 0.034 dB and is 500 times faster. | A moving mic re-matches every 40 ms, and the measurement took 70 ms. Recorded in the decision log. | Nothing to decide; the measured version stays in the tests as the reference. |
| C7 | The position pad shows the cap on the left, the cone's edge on the right, the closest capture at the top and the farthest at the bottom. A new mic starts at the cap, closest (0, 0). | Matches how most movable-mic UIs read; the real UI is Phase 11. | Flip the axes in `MicPositionPad`, change the parameter defaults. |
| C9 | Input calibration is on by default, with the interface's full scale at +12 dBu: the Solo 4th Gen instrument input's maximum level at minimum gain, from Focusrite's spec as I remember it (not checked against the manual). With the gain knob turned up, the real figure is lower by the knob's gain. | Calibration is what makes captures react to the guitar the way the real amp would; NAM's own plugin uses the same idea. | Turn it off by default, or change the default level once the Solo's manual (or a measurement) gives the real number. |
| C10 | A calibration change reloads every loaded capture once the setting has been still for 300 ms. | A reload is the only way to re-measure loudness with the new input level; reloading per knob step would flood the loader. | Shorter or longer settle time. |
| C8 | The processor's housekeeping timer now runs at 50 Hz (was 20 Hz) so a dragged mic re-morphs every 40 ms. It's a few microseconds of message-thread work per tick. | The plan's 40 ms rate needs ticks at least every 20 ms. | Lower it and accept slower re-morphing. |

## Phase 4: compressor and EQ

| # | Assumption | Why | To change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Both compressors start switched off; the EQs start on but flat (which is bit-transparent). The plan implies the pre compressor starts on. | With its default auto makeup (+9 dB at 4:1, -24 dB threshold) a compressor that's on in a fresh session pushes every amp noticeably harder before you've chosen to use it. The style presets (Phase 5) switch it on where the style wants it. | Flip the `comp_pre_on` default in `PluginProcessor.cpp`. |
| E2 | Auto makeup compensates the reduction at a -12 dBFS input (about where a DI's and the post-cab signal's peaks sit). | The plan says "auto-makeup" without a reference level; at 0 dBFS it would add far too much gain for guitar levels. | Change `autoMakeupReferenceDb`. |
| E3 | Auto release: a 60 ms release and a slow smoother (400 ms attack, 1.5 s release) side by side, the larger reduction winning. | A standard program-dependent design: short peaks recover fast, long squashing recovers slowly without pumping. The numbers are starting points. | The constants in `Compressor.h`. |
| E4 | Pedal mode ignores the attack knob (fixed 2 ms), uses the other knobs as they are, and keeps the mix knob. | "Fast fixed attack and heavy squash" in the plan; heavy squash is up to the ratio knob. | Fix the ratio too, or give Pedal its own defaults. |
| E5 | The RMS detector averages over 10 ms, and the sidechain high-pass is 12 dB/oct. | Common choices; the plan names neither. | Constants in `Compressor.h`. |
| E6 | Graphic EQ bands: Q 0.9, exact octaves (62.5 Hz shown as 63), midpoints weighted 0.5 in the design. | The only way found to meet the plan's +-1 dB (the plain method reached 1.05 dB at best). | `prototypes/graphic_eq.py` has the study; change `graphicQ` and the weight together. |
| E7 | Parametric bands: +-18 dB gain, Q 0.1 to 18, defaults 100 Hz low shelf, 400 Hz / 1 kHz / 3 kHz peaks, 8 kHz high shelf. | E9 | Effect order is changed in the basic panel with < and > buttons. | Drag to reorder is the Phase 11 GUI. | Phase 11. |

## Phase 5: delay, tempo, presets

| # | Assumption | Why | To change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | The delay's mix is equal-power: dry cos(mix x 90 degrees), wet sin(mix x 90 degrees). The reverb uses the same law. | The repeats are decorrelated from the dry, so equal power keeps the level steady across the knob. The plan says "dry/wet mix" without a law. | Keep the dry at unity and make the knob a wet level, pedal style. |
| D2 | Digital repeats pass a soft limiter that's exactly linear below 0.7 and approaches 1. | It's what keeps 110% feedback bounded. Normal repeats are untouched; only repeats above about -3 dBFS are gently compressed. | The knee constant in `Delay.cpp`. |
| D3 | Analog: an extra 3.5 kHz low-pass per pass and tanh saturation (drive 1.5). Tape: an extra 5 kHz low-pass, drive 1.2, wow 0.5 Hz +-1 ms, flutter 6 Hz +-0.08 ms (smoothed random). | Starting points for "darker, lightly saturated" and "wow and flutter, saturation, HF loss". Four repeats at 70% feedback lose 31 dB more at 6 kHz than at 500 Hz (analog). | Constants at the top of `Delay.cpp`; Sean tunes by ear. |
| D4 | Analog and tape time changes glide with a 150 ms one-pole, limited to 0.25 samples per sample (repeats bend at most about 5 semitones down or 4 up). | Unlimited, a 150 ms jump dived the repeats about 30 semitones. | `glideSeconds`, `maxGlideRate`. |
| D5 | Ducking reduces the repeats by up to the set amount, fully once the input's envelope reaches -20 dBFS (5 ms attack, 100 ms release). Default 6 dB. | "On by default at a moderate amount". The first design used absolute thresholds and stayed ducked for a second after playing stopped. | `duckReference` and the envelope times. |
| D6 | Delay defaults: digital, stereo layout, synced to a dotted eighth (375 ms at 120 BPM), feedback 35%, mix 25%, loop cuts 120 Hz and 6 kHz. Turning a loop cut to its end (20 Hz, 20 kHz) takes it out of the loop entirely. | The dotted eighth is the CHON/Polyphia staple. | Parameter defaults in `BlockParameters.cpp`. |
| D7 | Bypassed, the delay's repeats ring out (spillover) with feedback capped at 99%, so even a self-oscillating loop dies away; after 1 s below -100 dBFS it stops processing. | Spillover with 110% feedback would otherwise ring forever. | The cap in `Delay.cpp`. |
| D8 | Tap tempo: the average of the last 4 intervals; a tap more than 25% off the average is ignored as a stray, two agreeing strays in a row are a deliberate tempo change, 2 s of silence starts over, 30 to 300 BPM. The footswitch's tap is CC 80 (value 64 or more), changeable with the "Tap Tempo CC" parameter. GUI taps are timed to the start of the audio buffer (2.7 ms resolution). | The plan specifies averaging, outlier rejection, and the 2 s reset but not the numbers or the CC. | `TapTempo` constants; the `midi_tap_cc` parameter. |
| D9 | The tempo and the tap CC are saved in presets. | The plan lists tempo and MIDI mappings as preset contents. | Mark them global in `Presets.cpp`. |
| P1 | Basic presets store absolute file paths for captures and IRs. | Relative paths plus content hashes for relinking are the Phase 11 full system; format_version 1 is its starting point. | Phase 11. |
| P2 | Global settings left out of presets: input calibration on/off and the interface level. (A4 reference, tuner mute, and CPU fallback will join when they exist.) | The plan's global list. | `presets::isGlobal`. |
| P3 | Loading a preset while playing: 20 ms fade out, apply, then fade back in 150 ms after its loads finish (or after 5 s at most). Measured: about half a second of silence when a capture loads. | "A short fade (between songs, not seamless)". | `presetFadeSeconds`, `presetSettleMs`. |
| P4 | Presets live in `~/Library/Application Support/BellyDSP/presets` (`AmpSim` before the 2026-10-03 rename, DS37), one `.json` per preset, saved and loaded from the header's Save and Load buttons. | The plan's folder. A browser list is Phase 11. | `presets::defaultFolder`. |
| P5 | Every parameter starts at its default as read back through its normalized value (so an 8 kHz default is exactly 8000 Hz, not 7999.9995). | Makes a fresh processor and a preset-loaded one bit-identical. | Nothing to decide. |

## Phase 5: chorus and reverb

| # | Assumption | Why | To change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | The chorus mix is equal power (cos/sin), with the protected lows at unity. The first version was linear, as briefed. | Linear left the chorused band 3 dB down at 50% against the lows, which tilted the tone toward the bass. A sustained pure tone can swell up to 3 dB mid-way through a mix move under equal power; broadband guitar doesn't. | `Chorus::mixGains`. |
| H2 | Dimension is pure stereo width: the difference of an antiphase pair (as on the SDD-320), no pitch wobble (0.1 cent), and its wet cancels in mono (at 100% wet the mono sum is only the lows). At 50% mono is fine. | It's what makes Dimension sound like Dimension. A mono-safe version (voice A left, B right) wobbles 3 cents. | `Chorus::voiceSettings`. |
| H3 | Tri is three voices panned left, centre, right, not three per channel. | Three per channel average the delay sweep away and comb with the dry at -14 dB; panned gives -9.5 dB. | `Chorus::voiceSettings`. |
| H4 | Chorus low-end protection is a crossover: a 24 dB/oct high-pass feeds the delay lines and a 12 dB/oct low-pass carries the lows around them at unity. | Just cutting the lows from the wet drops the bass 6 dB at 50% mix; a 2nd-order high-pass let the low E wobble 5 cents. | `Chorus.cpp`. |
| H5 | Chorus base delays, depth ranges, and the default rate (0.8 Hz) are starting points; depth and rate glide over 100 ms; the random LFO restarts its sequence on a mode or shape change; the tanh isn't oversampled (it's light, and post-cab). | Defaults to tune by ear. | `Chorus.h` constants. |
| R1 | The FDN's Householder matrix is followed by a one-line rotation. | Plain Householder at 16 lines keeps 0.875 of each line on itself: a flutter echo. The rotation is still orthogonal and O(N). | `FeedbackDelayNetwork::feedbackMatrix`. |
| R2 | The plate's one-pole damping is replaced by low and high shelves in the tank (flat at the paper's settings, so the paper's plate is exact). | The paper's damping, fitted to a high-band T60, gave a 1.8 s mid T60 when 3.0 s was set. | `DattorroPlate`. |
| R3 | The plate's decay runs 6 to 20% long, worst at short decays with a low high multiplier; tests hold it to 25% (the FDN engines to the plan's 10%; they measure within 4.8%). | Its 725 ms loop means short decays happen mostly within one trip around it. Reported, not fudged. | Calibrate the plate's decay mapping against measured T60s. |
| R4 | The plate ignores Size and the early/late balance. | Dattorro's lengths are fixed, and a plate has no early reflections. | Scale the plate's lengths with Size (changes its character). |
| R5 | Size maps to 2^(3 size - 2) times the 30 to 100 ms line spread (0.25x to 2x), and is normalized out of the loudness; every engine's late reverb sits near the input's level at a 2 s decay; early reflections have unit energy and add on top at the balance's centre. | Keeps engine switches and Size moves from jumping in level. | `Reverb.h` constants. |
| R6 | Early reflections feed the network (they're its input), rather than running in parallel. | The plan's "multi-tap delay ahead of the network". | `Reverb.cpp`. |
| R7 | Freeze mutes only the reverb's input: what's already in the pre-delay still reaches the tank, so a note played just before the stomp is frozen too. | Feels right on a footswitch. | `Reverb.cpp`. |
| R8 | Bypassed, the reverb goes idle (costs nothing) after its tail stays below -120 dBFS for 1 s. | Longer than any internal path, so a bypass right after a note can't cut its pre-delayed tail. | `silenceHoldSeconds`. |
| R9 | Pre-delay changes crossfade two read heads over 30 ms (no glide, so no pitch bend). Range 0 to 500 ms; tempo sync is in the processor. | A gliding pre-delay sounds like a tape effect. | `Reverb.cpp`. |
| R10 | Reverb defaults: Hall, mix 25%, pre-delay 10 ms, size 50%, decay 2 s, low x1.2, high x0.5, diffusion 80%, modulation 30% at 0.5 Hz, wet cuts 100 Hz and 8 kHz, ducking off. Ducking reaches 18 dB at full above -30 dBFS. | Starting points. | Parameter defaults. |
| R11 | Reverb freeze is footswitchable on CC 81 (value 64 or more toggles it), changeable with "Reverb Freeze CC"; the footswitch takes effect in the same audio buffer and the switch in the GUI catches up from the timer. | The plan says footswitchable; the CC number is a guess at a free one. | `midi_freeze_cc`. |

## Phase 6: MIDI mappings

| # | Assumption | Why | To change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Mapped CCs are applied from the 50 Hz timer, so a mapped switch acts up to 20 ms after the press. Program change, tap tempo (CC 80), and freeze (CC 81) stay on the audio thread and act in the same buffer. | Setting a parameter notifies its listeners, which can lock, so it can't be done on the audio thread. 20 ms is about one 16th note at 190 BPM, but a block switching on that late is only heard as the block's own 10 ms fade-in. | Give each mapped block a lock-free override like freeze's (`freezeOverride`), or raise the timer rate. |
| M2 | A toggle flips on every value of 64 or more and ignores values below 64. | Covers both kinds of footswitch that leave the state to the app: momentary (127 on press, 0 on release) and trigger (127 on every press). A latching controller (it alternates 127 and 0 itself) would need two presses per change as a toggle; for that, set the mapping to momentary, which follows the switch. | `MidiMap::handle`. Task 6.M checks what Sean's footswitch sends. |
| M3 | MIDI learn makes a toggle for on/off parameters and a continuous mapping over the full range for everything else; choice parameters (modes) get continuous too, so a pedal steps through them. | The common cases: footswitch on a block, pedal on a knob. | `MidiMap::learn`. |
| M4 | One mapping per CC (learning a CC replaces its old mapping); one parameter can have several CCs. | Simplest model with no surprises about which mapping wins. | `MidiMap::set`. |
| M6 | MIDI learn lives on right-click (and ctrl-click) in the panel, with a "MIDI..." button listing every mapping. A switch's mapping can be changed between toggle and follow-the-switch from the same menu. | The plan's "click a control, press a switch, done", without a separate MIDI page before Phase 11. | `AmpSimEditor::midiMenuFor`. |
| M5 | Mappings are part of each preset as well as the app state; loading a preset that has no "midi" list keeps the current mappings. | BUILD_PLAN lists MIDI mappings under what a preset holds. Keeping mappings when a preset has none means presets made before mappings existed don't wipe the footswitch setup. | `presets::apply`. Moving mappings to global settings is a one-line change there plus removing them from `capture`. |

## Phase 6: gates

| # | Assumption | Why | To change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | "Threshold" is the open threshold; the close threshold is threshold minus hysteresis. | The usual gate convention. The catch: widening the hysteresis after Learn lowers the close threshold toward the noise. | `Gate.h`. |
| G2 | Learn's 6 dB margin is on the close threshold, so the open threshold lands 14 dB over the noise floor (with the default 8 dB hysteresis). Learn takes the 95th percentile of 2 s, not the maximum. | The close threshold decides whether noise can hold the gate open; the percentile keeps a stray glitch of up to 100 ms from raising it. | `Gate::learnMarginDb`, `learnPercentile`. |
| G3 | The release falls a constant number of dB per second (the knob is the time to fall 60 dB), so a full mute takes twice the knob time (to -120 dB). | "Release exponential in dB", read as a string's own decay. | `Gate::releaseCoefficient`. |
| G4 | One release knob: in adaptive mode it sets the slow side (250 ms) and stops get a fixed 20 ms. Anything dying faster than about 150 dB/s from playing level counts as a stop, palm mutes included. | Tight stops shouldn't need tuning, and it suits chugs. A heavily damped note will cut fast too. | `Gate::adaptiveFastReleaseMs`, the 8 and 20 dB gap thresholds. |
| G5 | Hold defaults to 10 ms: choked tremolo gaps of 20 ms (E4) to 26 ms (A2) ride through, and a hard stop is still silent in 60 to 80 ms. | The plan's "hold to bridge tremolo gaps", against tight stops. | `gate_*_hold` default. |
| G6 | (Superseded by PT13: -45 dBFS since 2026-10-04.) The default threshold is -55 dBFS. With 8 dB of hysteresis it closes only on noise whose 10 ms peak stays under -63 dBFS (about -70 dBFS RMS); on a noisier DI (-65 dBFS RMS hiss peaks near -58) the gate stays open until Learn is pressed. | Safe for a typical DI at minimum interface gain; Learn is the intended workflow. | Parameter default, or run Learn at first launch. |
| G7 | The sidechain high-pass is 24 dB/oct at 100 Hz (the compressor's is 12). Soft low notes around -40 dBFS open 2 to 3 ms after they start; picks open at once. | 12 dB/oct takes only 9.4 dB off 60 Hz hum, not enough to keep it from holding the gate open. | `Gate::designSidechain`. |
| G8 | A gate starts open after prepare or reset and closes through its release, so re-enabling it fades in from exactly the dry signal. Cost: about 0.4 s of noise fading out at startup. | No jump when a gate is switched on mid-song. | `Gate::resetState`. |
| G9 | Both gates start switched off, with Gate B linked. | Like every effect, a fresh session is just the amp and cab, bit for bit. | `gate_a_on`, `gate_b_on` defaults. |
| G10 | Gate A keeps running its detector while it's off (about 1 us a buffer), so a linked Gate B follows it and Learn works with it off. | The plan says Gate B follows Gate A's detector; a switched-off Gate A shouldn't leave Gate B with a stale curve. | `GateA` in `LinkedGates.h`. |
| G11 | Gate B's panel offers own-input detection too (the plan only names Gate A). Linked, Gate B's own knobs are dimmed but still editable. Learn sets Gate B's threshold only when it's on and unlinked. | Unlinked, an independent Gate B may want to see the amp's output; there's no reason to hide it. | `GatePanel`. |
| G12 | A saved section order that lacks a newer block gets it where it breaks the fewest pairs of the default order (latest such place on a tie). A Phase 5 pre order "eq, comp" becomes "gate, eq, comp". | Puts new blocks where they belong without undoing the player's own reordering. | `AmpSimProcessor::setSectionOrder`. |
