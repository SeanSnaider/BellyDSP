# Assumptions to review

Sean asked on 2026-10-01 for the whole build to run to completion without stopping for questions, with every assumption written down here so we can go through them once the program is done. Each entry says what was assumed, why, and what changing it would take. Nothing here is final: the decision log in `BUILD_PLAN.md` records what's been settled, and this file records what Claude decided on Sean's behalf.

Status key: **Open** (waiting for Sean), **Confirmed** (Sean agreed), **Changed** (Sean chose differently; the change is noted).

## Phase 3: cab packs (movable mics)

| # | Assumption | Why | To change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | File-name keywords place IRs across the speaker: Cap / Center 0, CapEdge 0.25, CapCone 0.4, Cone 0.6, Edge 1.0. Distances read from `1in`, `2.5cm`, `10mm` and are scaled so the closest capture is 0 and the farthest 1. | The common naming conventions in IR packs; the exact numbers are a guess at how far apart those positions are. | Edit the keyword table in `CabPack.cpp`, or put a `cabpack.json` in the pack folder, which overrides names entirely. |
| C2 | If names don't say where every mic was (or two files land on one spot), the files are laid out left to right in name order, as a 1D slider, and the status says so. | Better than refusing the pack. | Refuse such packs instead. |
| C3 | A pack uses each IR's first (left) channel; the close mic's left/right setting applies only to single IR files. The room mic doesn't take packs. | Packs are close-mic captures; a stereo room with positions isn't in the plan. | Per-pack channel choice is easy to add. |
| C4 | All IRs in a pack must share one sample rate; a pack that mixes rates is refused with a message. | Morphing needs a common frequency grid. Resampling at load is possible but wasn't worth it without real packs to test. | Resample at load. |
| C5 | Minimum-phase reconstruction floors the spectrum 120 dB below its peak and gives the DC and Nyquist bins their neighbours' values. | Real cab IRs have near-exact zeros at DC and Nyquist, which otherwise leave a -60 dB error spread across the morphed IR; with the fix it's -77 dB. Neither change is audible (0 Hz, 24 kHz, -120 dB). | Nothing to decide unless a real pack misbehaves. |
| C6 | IR loudness matching is now computed exactly from the IR's K-weighted energy instead of measured with 4 s of white noise. It agrees with the measurement within 0.034 dB and is 500 times faster. | A moving mic re-matches every 40 ms, and the measurement took 70 ms. Recorded in the decision log. | Nothing to decide; the measured version stays in the tests as the reference. |
| C7 | The position pad shows the cap on the left, the cone's edge on the right, the closest capture at the top and the farthest at the bottom. A new mic starts at the cap, closest (0, 0). | Matches how most movable-mic UIs read; the real UI is Phase 11. | Flip the axes in `MicPositionPad`, change the parameter defaults. |
| C9 | Input calibration is on by default, with the interface's full scale at +12 dBu: the Solo 4th Gen instrument input's maximum level at minimum gain, from Focusrite's spec as I remember it (not checked against the manual). With the gain knob turned up, the real figure is lower by the knob's gain. | Calibration is what makes captures react to the guitar the way the real amp would; NAM's own plugin uses the same idea. | Turn it off by default, or change the default level once the Solo's manual (or a measurement) gives the real number. |
| C10 | A calibration change reloads every loaded capture once the setting has been still for 300 ms. | A reload is the only way to re-measure loudness with the new input level; reloading per knob step would flood the loader. | Shorter or longer settle time. |
| C8 | The processor's housekeeping timer now runs at 50 Hz (was 20 Hz) so a dragged mic re-morphs every 40 ms. It's a few microseconds of message-thread work per tick. | The plan's 40 ms rate needs ticks at least every 20 ms. | Lower it and accept slower re-morphing. |

## Phase 4: compressor and EQ

| # | Assumption | Why | To change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Both compressors start switched off; the EQs start on but flat (which is bit-transparent). The plan implies the pre compressor starts on. | With its default auto makeup (+9 dB at 4:1, -24 dB threshold) a compressor that's on in a fresh session pushes every amp noticeably harder before you've chosen to use it. The style presets (Phase 5) switch it on where the style wants it. | Flip the `comp_pre_on` default in `PluginProcessor.cpp`. |
| E2 | Auto makeup compensates the reduction at a -12 dBFS input (about where a DI's and the post-cab signal's peaks sit). | The plan says "auto-makeup" without a reference level; at 0 dBFS it would add far too much gain for guitar levels. | Change `autoMakeupReferenceDb`. |
| E3 | Auto release: a 60 ms release and a slow smoother (400 ms attack, 1.5 s release) side by side, the larger reduction winning. | A standard program-dependent design: short peaks recover fast, long squashing recovers slowly without pumping. The numbers are starting points. | The constants in `Compressor.h`. |
| E4 | Pedal mode ignores the attack knob (fixed 2 ms), uses the other knobs as they are, and keeps the mix knob. | "Fast fixed attack and heavy squash" in the plan; heavy squash is up to the ratio knob. | Fix the ratio too, or give Pedal its own defaults. |
| E5 | The RMS detector averages over 10 ms, and the sidechain high-pass is 12 dB/oct. | Common choices; the plan names neither. | Constants in `Compressor.h`. |
| E6 | Graphic EQ bands: Q 0.9, exact octaves (62.5 Hz shown as 63), midpoints weighted 0.5 in the design. | The only way found to meet the plan's +-1 dB (the plain method reached 1.05 dB at best). | `prototypes/graphic_eq.py` has the study; change `graphicQ` and the weight together. |
| E7 | Parametric bands: +-18 dB gain, Q 0.1 to 18, defaults 100 Hz low shelf, 400 Hz / 1 kHz / 3 kHz peaks, 8 kHz high shelf. Cuts: low 20 Hz to 1 kHz (default 80 Hz), high 1 to 20 kHz (default 12 kHz). | Usual parametric EQ ranges; the plan doesn't give them. | `BlockParameters.cpp` and `Equalizer.h`. |
| E8 | Reordering a section, changing a band's type, and changing a cut's slope dip to the unprocessed signal for 10 ms each way rather than crossfading. Switching EQ modes does crossfade (both banks run during the fade). | A true crossfade between two orders or two filter types needs a second copy of the blocks; the dip is click-free, but a heavily processed sound is briefly unprocessed. | Duplicate the blocks and crossfade, at twice the CPU during changes. |
| E9 | Effect order is changed in the basic panel with < and > buttons. | Drag to reorder is the Phase 11 GUI. | Phase 11. |

## Phase 5: delay, tempo, presets

| # | Assumption | Why | To change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | The delay's mix is equal-power: dry cos(mix x 90 degrees), wet sin(mix x 90 degrees). The reverb uses the same law. | The repeats are decorrelated from the dry, so equal power keeps the level steady across the knob. The plan says "dry/wet mix" without a law. | Keep the dry at unity and make the knob a wet level, pedal style. |
| D2 | Digital repeats pass a soft limiter that's exactly linear below 0.7 and approaches 1. | It's what keeps 110% feedback bounded. Normal repeats are untouched; only repeats above about -3 dBFS are gently compressed. | The knee constant in `Delay.cpp`. |
| D3 | Analog: an extra 3.5 kHz low-pass per pass and tanh saturation (drive 1.5). Tape: an extra 5 kHz low-pass, drive 1.2, wow 0.5 Hz +-1 ms, flutter 6 Hz +-0.08 ms (smoothed random). | Starting points for "darker, lightly saturated" and "wow and flutter, saturation, HF loss". Four repeats at 70% feedback lose 31 dB more at 6 kHz than at 500 Hz (analog). | Constants at the top of `Delay.cpp`; Sean tunes by ear. |
| D4 | Analog and tape time changes glide with a 150 ms one-pole, limited to 0.25 samples per sample (repeats bend at most about 5 semitones down or 4 up). | Unlimited, a 150 ms jump dived the repeats about 30 semitones. | `glideSeconds`, `maxGlideRate`. |
| D5 | Ducking reduces the repeats by up to the set amount, fully once the input's envelope reaches -20 dBFS (5 ms attack, 100 ms release). Default 6 dB. | "On by default at a moderate amount". The first design used absolute thresholds and stayed ducked for a second after playing stopped. | `duckReference` and the envelope times. |
| D6 | Delay defaults: digital, stereo layout, synced to a dotted eighth (375 ms at 120 BPM), feedback 35%, mix 25%, loop cuts 120 Hz and 6 kHz. Turning a loop cut to its end (20 Hz, 20 kHz) takes it out of the loop entirely. | The dotted eighth is the CHON/Polyphia staple. | Parameter defaults in `BlockParameters.cpp`. |
| D7 | Bypassed, the delay's repeats ring out (spillover) with feedback capped at 99%, so even a self-oscillating loop dies away; after 1 s below -100 dBFS it stops processing. | Spillover with 110% feedback would otherwise ring forever. | The cap in `Delay.cpp`. |
| D8 | Tap tempo: the average of the last 4 intervals; a tap more than 25% off the average is ignored as a stray, two agreeing strays in a row are a deliberate tempo change, 2 s of silence starts over, 30 to 300 BPM. The footswitch's tap is CC 80 (value 64 or more), changeable with the "Tap Tempo CC" parameter. GUI taps are timed to the start of the audio buffer (2.7 ms resolution). | The plan specifies averaging, outlier rejection, and the 2 s reset but not the numbers or the CC. | `TapTempo` constants; the `midi_tap_cc` parameter. |
| D9 | The tempo and the tap CC are saved in presets. | The plan lists tempo and MIDI mappings as preset contents. | Mark them global in `Presets.cpp`. |
| P1 | Basic presets store absolute file paths for captures and IRs. | Relative paths plus content hashes for relinking are the Phase 11 full system; format_version 1 is its starting point. | Phase 11. |
| P2 | Global settings left out of presets: input calibration on/off and the interface level. (A4 reference, tuner mute, and CPU fallback will join when they exist.) | The plan's global list. | `presets::isGlobal`. |
| P3 | Loading a preset while playing: 20 ms fade out, apply, then fade back in 150 ms after its loads finish (or after 5 s at most). Measured: about half a second of silence when a capture loads. | "A short fade (between songs, not seamless)". | `presetFadeSeconds`, `presetSettleMs`. |
| P4 | Presets live in `~/Library/Application Support/AmpSim/presets`, one `.json` per preset, saved and loaded from the header's Save and Load buttons. | The plan's folder. A browser list is Phase 11. | `presets::defaultFolder`. |
| P5 | Every parameter starts at its default as read back through its normalized value (so an 8 kHz default is exactly 8000 Hz, not 7999.9995). | Makes a fresh processor and a preset-loaded one bit-identical. | Nothing to decide. |

## Phase 6: boost and overdrive

| # | Assumption | Why | To change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | The drive circuits take 0 dBFS as 4.3611 V peak (+12 dBu RMS), the same interface calibration as the NAM slots (C9). A unity-gain circuit is unity in the chain. | Diodes clip at ~0.6 V, so the circuits need real voltages; the Solo's input calibration is the best number available. | `voltsAtFullScale` in each block's Settings; the processor can pass the global interface level. |
| V2 | Transistor buffers (the Tube Screamer's emitter followers, the RAT's JFET follower) are ideal unity buffers, and diodes are Shockley 1N914s (Is 2.52 nA, N 1.752) without series resistance or capacitance. | The brief's simulator spec; the buffers' few tenths of a dB and the junction capacitances matter little in the audio band. | Add the parts to `prototypes/circuits.py` first, then to the models. |
| V3 | The LM308: slew rate 0.3 V/us (ElectroSmash), gain-bandwidth 0.92 MHz from a BJT input pair's tanh at that slew rate, DC gain 300 V/mV (datasheet), output 1.5 V short of each 9 V rail (+-3.0 V). | The datasheet's curves aren't readable here; these are consistent with ElectroSmash's "flat to about 500 Hz at 67 dB of gain" and the datasheet's 1-2 V output swing. | `LM308` in `prototypes/circuits.py` and the constants in `DistortionCircuit`, then re-render the fixtures. |
| V4 | The Tube Screamer's op-amps are ideal (no rails, no bandwidth). | In every fixture their outputs peak at 1.4 V at most, far inside a 4558's swing on 9 V. | Give them the macromodel too, as with the LM308. |
| V5 | Pot tapers: audio taper is (81^p - 1) / 80 (10% at half rotation) for the TS Drive and the RAT's Distortion and Filter; the TS Tone is linear; the RAT's Filter turns the other way so tone 1 is brightest. | The usual audio-taper midpoint; the parts lists name A, B, or "G" tapers without curves. | `audioTaper` in `DriveCircuits.cpp`. |
| V6 | The TS808's output side: C7 1 uF, 1k, Level 100k at maximum, C8 0.1 uF into the output buffer's 510k bias, RB 100, C9 10 uF, RC 10k; the tone stage per Yeh's Fig. 15 (a 10k bias on IC1b's input). | ElectroSmash's text and parts list disagree on some designators; this follows the parts and Yeh's circuit, and only sub-5 Hz corners depend on it. | The netlist in `prototypes/circuits.py` and `MidDriveCircuit`. |
| V7 | Each pedal drives a 1 MOhm load (an amp's input), and its volume pot sits at maximum; Level is a dB gain after it (-60 to +24 dB on the Overdrive, -24 to +24 on the Boost). | The real next stage is the digital chain; a volume knob as a pot would only add a taper to a gain. | The `load` constants; Level ranges in `DriveEngine::setSettings` and `Boost::setSettings`. |
| V8 | Mix is linear, (1 - mix) dry + mix wet, with Level after it, and the dry is delayed by exactly the oversampler's own response (4.2 samples at 4x) so the blend doesn't comb-filter. | An unaligned dry would notch the blend near 5.7 kHz (measured: a 6.5 dB dip at 50%). | `DriveEngine::processChunk`. |
| V9 | Tight is a 12 dB/oct Butterworth high-pass on the wet path only, off at 20 Hz, up to 2 kHz; the dry for Mix stays full range. | A parallel blend usually keeps the clean low end; the plan doesn't say. | `DriveEngine::updateControls`. |
| V10 | Boost Clean's tilt is a 1 kHz high shelf (Q 0.5) of the tilt with the whole turned down by half, -12 to +12 dB (highs minus lows). Tight's defaults are a 150 Hz high-pass (20 Hz to 1 kHz) and +6 dB (0 to 12) at 800 Hz, Q 0.7. | The plan names the controls but not the numbers; 800 Hz is where the Tube Screamer's own response peaks. | Constants and defaults in `Boost.h`. |
| V11 | Boost Screamer is fixed at drive 0 and tone noon and ignores the tight frequency, so it's the classic pedal; extra tightness comes from the Overdrive's Tight or the Boost's Tight mode. | "Where the mode uses them" in the plan; a pre-high-pass in front of the TS would change its character. | `Boost::setSettings`. |
| V12 | Mode switches take 100 ms: the new mode runs unheard for 80 ms from rest, then a 20 ms equal-power crossfade. A 4x/8x change dips to silence for about 10 ms. | A cold circuit clips around the wrong bias while its coupling capacitors charge; a mode change isn't a seamless amp switch. | `DriveEngine::warmupSeconds`; keep every circuit running (2x CPU per extra mode) for instant switches. |
| V13 | The aliasing limit is -50 dB of inharmonic energy at 4x for full-drive tones up to 1.3 kHz at -12 dBFS. Higher tones alias more (down to -16 dB at 10 kHz on Distortion at 4x, -32 at 8x). | Guitar fundamentals stop near 1.3 kHz and their harmonics are far weaker than a -12 dBFS sine; a full-drive clipper flips in less than one sample at 192 kHz. | 8x (1.5 to 1.9x the CPU), or antiderivative antialiasing for the diode stages. Sean listens first (6.L). |
| V14 | When the chain re-enables a bypassed drive block it calls reset(), so the circuit starts cold under the chain's 10 ms fade (the same settling the warm-up avoids for mode switches). | `Chain` is outside this task's scope. | Let the drive blocks keep running while bypassed, or warm up after reset. |
| V15 | Defaults: Boost Clean at 0 dB (bit-transparent); Overdrive Mid Drive, drive and tone at noon, level 0 dB, mix 100%, tight off; 4x. | Starting points; the processor will decide what's on by default. | Settings defaults in `Boost.h` and `Overdrive.h`. |
